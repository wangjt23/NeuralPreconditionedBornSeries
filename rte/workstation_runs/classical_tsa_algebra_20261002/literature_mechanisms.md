# Three mechanisms after the negative tail result

Primary-source check dated 2026-10-02. This ranking concerns the next inexpensive discriminator, not a demonstrated winner or a novelty claim. No compute, training, remote write, or new dataset was performed for this assessment.

The completed development diagnostic reports roughly 4.5 TSA16 classical calls per full solve, no mean call reduction from the neural insertion at N128/N256, and slower four-forward averaging. Both physical and useful projected directions were audited. The problem is therefore not simply an acceptance bug or wasted norm inside old AZ. A new mechanism must reduce the expensive repeated action, remove materially more remaining work, or improve its implementation. Existing September work already implemented cached complex-linear CT/NTO factors; NTO-HSS2's initial speed hypothesis failed. Expanded-depth classical HSS removed the old CT/SCT advantage. Flat rank-eight teacher-error POD also failed to reduce iterations. These negative controls constrain the proposals below.

For an ambitious physics/mathematics study, rank 3 could become more significant in a demonstrably stiff/strong-anisotropy regime. That is a conditional change of workload requiring explicit evidence; it is not supported by the current fixed L12/g0.65/epsilon0.1 fast classical cohort. Rank 2 addresses the present cost obstacle directly, but is less mature than rank 1 and must differ concretely from prior CT/NTO attempts.

## Current primary evidence and applicability limits

- **Cached linear action is an established design.** [Jia, Liu, Wang, Xu, McMg, 2606.30495v2, 2026-07-02](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.30495v2) computes nonlinear medium features in setup, caches them, and applies residual-linear multichannel smoothing/transfer. Its main Helmholtz experiments use finite differences and absorbing layers; a Fourier-spectral appendix is present. It compares tuned Born/shifted-Laplacian/LU controls and reports wall time, while also showing that fewer GMRES iterations need not make the solve faster. This supports the setup/apply split and phase-space coarse representation, not transport convergence or the present one-RHS amortization threshold. Its stated residual linearity should not be silently strengthened to our complex-linearity requirement. The RTE repository already uses a similar split.

- **Multilevel structure can lose badly on cost.** [Zhang, Li, Saad, GMP, 2607.28456v1, 2026-07-30](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.28456v1) learns smoothers/transfers/coarse correction using an AMG hierarchy for general sparse matrices. Main experiments train per matrix; published median setup/solve times favor successful ILUT over GMP by large margins despite learned convergence improvements elsewhere. Its shared-model scale-transfer demonstration concerns small anisotropic-diffusion matrices. It requires assembled sparse operator/hierarchy access. The RTE project primarily applies A through FFTs and also implements exact sparse spectral assembly in `spectral_sparse_reference.py`; neither route supplies an already demonstrated scalable AMG hierarchy. Assembly/storage, graph density, auxiliary-operator consistency, hierarchy setup, and transport suitability need their own checks. GMP is a useful overhead/failure warning, not a directly applicable recommended solver.

- **Compressed inverse representation is a hypothesis to diagnose.** [Xu, Li, Xi, Neural Approximate Inverse Preconditioners, 2510.13034v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13034v1) converts a learned Green kernel to sparse or hierarchical form. Examples are one-/two-dimensional convection-reaction-diffusion with Dirichlet conditions; the paper explicitly emphasizes iterations/spectra rather than wall-clock timings. It supplies a representation idea, not RTE evidence that fixed rank or elliptic locality survives grazing kinetic channels.

- **Closest transport coarse competitors remain classical and ROM-based.** [Fan, An, Ying](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.03061) use angular reduction with FFT homogeneous solves and heterogeneous recursive skeletonization. [Dölz, Palii, Schlottbom](https://arxiv.org/html/2102.09038v2) combine anisotropic angular subspace correction and compressed scattering under their variational/discretization assumptions. [Peng FGMRES–ROMSAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08735v3) exploits kinetic-correction low rank and affine parameter dependence; non-affine material variation complicates cheap reduced assembly. [Tang–Peng trajectory-aware ROM](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05001v1) addresses deployed-residual mismatch. None of these supplies a transferable convergence proof for arbitrary learned Fourier/S1 coarse factors.

- **Classical replay is feasible in principle on the installed toolkit.** [NVIDIA cuFFT CUDA 12.4.1 documentation, CUDA Graphs support](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/archive/12.4.1/cufft/index.html#cuda-graphs-support) supports graph capture of single-GPU FFT plans subject to stream-capture requirements and callback restrictions. This confirms an engineering option on the existing CUDA generation; it does not guarantee PyTorch capture compatibility, numerical parity, or a speedup for this exact code. Capture the fixed preconditioner block first; dynamic Krylov stopping and scalar host reads are a different problem. No toolkit upgrade or custom FFT callback is required for the first discriminator.

The source audit matters: `transport_baselines.py` repeatedly composes streaming inversion, the full-angle homogeneous reference, and physical defect actions; `models.py` already caches reference inverses but reconstructs some symbols/conversions on calls. `compiled_hss.py`, `collision_transport.py`, and `neural_transport_operator.py` already have valid material caches. `multilevel_neural_transport.py` explicitly uses a rediscretized coarse operator, whereas separate Galerkin controls exist. Therefore blanket advice to cache everything or use a coarse grid would repeat existing work.

Final source-access qualification: exact sparse spectral assembly is available through `spectral_sparse_reference.py`. The graph-AMG concern above is the absence of a ready, demonstrated scalable hierarchy and its unmeasured construction/application costs, not an impossibility of obtaining sparse operator entries.

## Common decision rule

Use the original FP64 physical residual and the evaluated solution-error checks. Time full unchanged solves on all development media, including those already easy, with the exact frozen classical portfolio. Distinguish engineering parity from algorithmic changes and distinguish reused development evidence from untouched test data. Preserve failures and negative results. Reserve seed 9626 until a candidate passes a predeclared development gate.

For Q distinct RHS per fixed medium, compare T_offline + S_candidate + Q t_candidate against S_classical + Q t_classical, including training/data, material setup, capture/loading, transfers, inference, and every action. Where t_candidate < t_classical, the approximate break-even count is max(0, T_offline + S_candidate - S_classical)/(t_classical - t_candidate). If online saving is nonpositive, extra training cannot create an online amortization argument. Credit repeated-source reuse to classical factors and Krylov recycling too.

If shared training serves multiple media, sum the setup/solve terms over those media and charge the offline total once; do not charge the entire training campaign independently to each medium or omit it entirely.

Recommended order is an unchanged-action cost discriminator, then a replacement-action feasibility check, then a transport coarse-space oracle if the first two leave headroom. This order limits work while leaving the substantive learned and physical mechanisms open.
