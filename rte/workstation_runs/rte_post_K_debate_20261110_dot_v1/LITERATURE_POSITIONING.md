# Literature positioning for the bounded second-injection control

Read-only targeted check,10 November2026. This is a short positioning audit, not an exhaustive novelty search. It does not change the numerical gates in NEXT_COUPLING_PLAN.md or authorize a new experiment. Repeated coarse correction and augmented/flexible Krylov iteration are established ideas. The proposed second injection is a classical mechanism control, not an originality claim.

## Verified primary precedents

1. R. E. Alcouffe, *Diffusion Synthetic Acceleration Methods for the Diamond-Differenced Discrete-Ordinates Equations*, Nuclear Science and Engineering64(2),344-355(1977). The publisher abstract describes alternating transport and diffusion solves coupled through correction terms and stresses compatible discretization for stability. Thus repeated low/high-order correction is not a new conceptual starting point. [Publisher record and abstract](https://wx1.ans.org/pubs/journals/nse/a_27375), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.13182/NSE77-1).

2. G. L. Ramone, M. L. Adams and P. F. Nowak, *A Transport Synthetic Acceleration Method for Transport Iterations*, Nuclear Science and Engineering125(3),257-283(1997). The indexed publisher abstract describes a transport sweep followed by a simplified transport correction, with bounded low-order iterations. It analyzes stability and spatial-discretization effects for its isotropic-scattering setting. This is a direct precedent for paying limited inner transport work to accelerate outer transport iteration. The repository's label TSA64 does not by itself establish that it implements this paper's particular method. [Publisher DOI/abstract](https://doi.org/10.13182/NSE97-A24274), [original1997 issue contents](https://wx1.ans.org/pubs/journals/download/a_24272).

3. Y. Saad, *A Flexible Inner-Outer Preconditioned GMRES Algorithm*, SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing14(2)(1993). FGMRES permits iteration-dependent preconditioning, including iterative methods used as inner preconditioners. Paying an additional inner solve and retaining the resulting search direction therefore falls within an established framework. [Original publisher abstract](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0914028).

4. A. Chapman and Y. Saad, *Deflated and Augmented Krylov Subspace Techniques*, Numerical Linear Algebra with Applications4(1),43-66(1997). Their framework includes augmented/deflated GMRES and inner-outer FGMRES. It is the appropriate conceptual family for additional coarse search directions. Our residual-conditioned injection is not automatically identical to a specific fixed-projector deflation algorithm; any such equivalence would need an algebraic derivation. [Authors' institutional publication record and abstract](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/deflated-and-augmented-krylov-subspace-techniques/).

5. Z. Peng, *A flexible GMRES solver with reduced order model enhanced synthetic acceleration preconditioner for parametric radiative transfer equation*, Journal of Computational Physics534,114004(2025). The accessible author manuscript, especially§4.1-4.3, already uses kinetic-correction ROMs in the first few iterations followed by DSA, within FGMRES. It also distinguishes correction-equation data from ordinary solution snapshots. Switching or repeating correction steps is consequently insufficient as a novelty claim. Its offline-trained upwind-DG/transport formulation is not a direct implementation match to this stored periodic Fourier operator or the present four-source accounting. [Original author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.08735v2), [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2025.114004).

6. N. Tang and Z. Peng, *Synthetic Acceleration Preconditioners for Parametric Radiative Transfer Equations based on Trajectory-Aware Reduced Order Models*, arXiv2509.05001(2025). The introduction and§3.4/4.2 directly address loss of efficiency after the first correction caused by offline/online residual-trajectory mismatch. They construct correction spaces sequentially using earlier corrected trajectories. Crucially, the manuscript distinguishes that construction from merely applying multiple ROMs. This is close prior art for future residual-derived or iteration-dependent spaces, not just for the current scheduling control. Our fixed untrained P8 factor reused twice is a simpler diagnostic, not a reproduction or claimed advance over that algorithm. [Original manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.05001v1).

A further relevant boundary is N. Liu and Z. Peng's2026 preprint on offline-free, on-the-fly ROM acceleration during implicit RTE time stepping. Only its author abstract was checked here. It already combines streaming data with evolving initial-guess/preconditioner approximations, so a future online mode-selection claim must distinguish its task and information/cost model. It is not a benchmark for this steady four-source problem. [Author abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19647).

Access scope: the older classical items above were checked through their primary publisher or author-institution abstracts/metadata; the two closest2024/2025 manuscripts were inspected in full-text HTML at the cited sections. No paywall was bypassed. No absence-of-search-results argument is used.

## What the present work can and cannot contribute

The following are NOT new by themselves: two-level correction, using an inner iterative solver in FGMRES, adding a second coarse direction, Fourier separation on a layered background, Woodbury/Schur complements, or residual-based iterative refinement. Nor does a different implementation detail or an unreported combination establish novelty.

P8 here means a truncation of tangential SPATIAL Fourier modes. All12 angular components remain in the coarse solve. The internal angular Fourier transformation used to factor each background block is a different operation and must not be conflated with P8 or described as an angular reduced-order basis.

The actual driver also distinguishes inputs: its classical preconditioner receives the current Arnoldi vector, while inject receives the current original residual and iterate. The proposal then joins the retained search space and a least-squares update, rather than simply assigning x plus the coarse correction. Peng's FGMRES-preconditioner equations use Arnoldi-vector inputs; they are not an algebraically identical residual-injection implementation. Even exact coarse-projector cancellation cannot simply be asserted for our tolerance-truncated inner solve followed by augmented least squares. Measure the resulting P8 residual; do not presume it vanishes.

The current potentially useful work is narrower and must be established on its own evidence:

- An explicit implementation for the actual stored operator, including its anisotropic full12-angle collision, finite-precision directions/weights, normal Nyquist and layer geometry. The near-cyclic angular representation has a real stored-data defect. It is not replaced by an idealized exactly circulant model.
- Original-G residual correction and independent original-A0/full-A acceptance with all setup, validation, conversion and online costs paid. Finite vector qualifications show observed reliability; they are not a uniform operator-norm guarantee. A rigorous applicability/correction bound would require additional work. Standard iterative-refinement reasoning alone is not a new theorem.
- A bounded applicability map: the initial constructed scene, its predeclared amplitude perturbation, exact-source N256 scale extension and K's background-preserving discrete transverse permutation. These do not constitute general material performance or unlock the old held-out protocol. K shows why raw coefficient-energy coverage cannot alone predict inner difficulty.
- A reproducible cost boundary against strong classical methods, including first-source versus fixed-four-source economics and failed implementations. The current small CPU advantage must not obscure the separate matched GPU result where compact4 was faster than the batched candidate. A CPU-only positive result supplies no GPU speedup claim.

These are candidate contributions to reproducibility, solver implementation and quantified scope. Whether any part is mathematically or algorithmically original remains open and would require a dedicated comparison with relevant spectral/block transport solvers and the modern correction/ROM literature. This short check is insufficient to assert priority.

## Consequence for the next gate

Keep the existing small fixed(1,2) second-injection plan. Its purpose is to distinguish whether repeated use of the same verified P8 factor can remove actual later classical work at a favorable fully paid cost. It is an attribution/control experiment within classical augmentation, not a new method merely because it adds another injection. No extra literature-driven sweep, parameter tuning, source expansion, GPU restart or learned trajectory model is introduced.

A success would establish that this particular classical scheduling choice pays on K under the frozen contract. A failure would identify an economic or mechanism limitation of that schedule. Neither outcome by itself proves originality or settles the value of trajectory-aware reduced spaces. Preserve the original one-shot E and native/compact controls and the narrow interpretation in the plan.

For a later separate comparison, a standard multiplicative control with classical action B and exact coarse correction C is z=Bv+C(v-ABv), giving residual propagation(I-AC)(I-AB). It is different from merely adding a coarse direction to the retained Krylov space. Exact coarse/projector formulas must not be assumed for the present tolerance-truncated inner solve, whose action can depend on its RHS. Related recycling/augmentation theory explicitly separates an augmented search space from projection through an orthonormal image basis; see [Parks et al., Recycling Krylov Subspaces for Sequences of Linear Systems, equations2.5-2.10](https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/bitstreams/00c3845d-2805-4731-b2da-53456a824b5a/download). This is context for a possible future strong control, not an extra arm added to the current small plan.
