For recorded one-source cost, let C* be the smallest successful total among all five classical arms in the same context. A candidate with setup S, additional per-material/source acquisition D, and complete warm accepted solve T has saving at least eta only if

    T <= (1-eta) C* - S - D.

T includes proposal inference, physical images, projections, continuation, and independent physical/error/finite acceptance. D cannot be amortized over an assumed source reuse count. The auditor reports the right side with D=0 at eta=0,5%,10% for the selected backend's measured setup. This is an optimistic allowance for the entire warm solve; it is neither a proposal-only budget nor evidence that any coefficient map attains it. A negative allowance rules out that particular recorded setup/cost model even at zero warm cost. A positive allowance needs an actual accuracy-preserving warm solver and all acquisition/inference charges before it constitutes headroom.

Setup reduction alone has no universal effect on learning feasibility. If only the classical comparator's setup falls by Delta and the candidate retains its old setup, its allowance shrinks by (1-eta)Delta. If both share the same improved setup, their common setup falls by Delta and the allowed candidate warm cost rises by eta Delta: the relative target becomes easier because less common overhead dominates the denominator. Thus neither 'lower classical setup proves learned room' nor 'lower setup always eliminates learned room' follows. Full raw timing, the candidate's actual setup/cost sharing, and the stronger denominator decide.
